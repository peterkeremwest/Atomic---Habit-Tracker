# v0.1.5 — What happened, in plain language

At v0.1 the company existed only on paper: a name, a business plan and a rulebook, with no building. Now the first storefront is open. It is a small shop that fits in your pocket, rented from an outside storefront-leasing company (Cloudflare) that has shopfronts in every city. The blueprints are kept in the records room (GitHub). Every time a new set is filed there, the leasing company's own construction crew (Workers Builds) rebuilds the shop front within about three minutes, without anyone having to call them. Only the shop floor, the `public` folder, is open to customers. The rulebook and the history log stay behind a locked door.

Inside, the shop works without a connection to anywhere else. Everything a customer writes goes into a filing drawer at their own desk (IndexedDB), and a copy of every change is dropped in an outgoing-mail tray for a head office that doesn't exist yet. The receptionist keeps a shelf of photocopies (the service worker), so the shop still opens when the phone is offline. The sign above the door, a typed-out ATOMIC with a blinking cursor, turns into a warning sign whenever the shop has lost its connection to the outside world. The lobby's paint scheme can be switched between green, amber and red.

The counter is organized like a well-run front desk. There's one day's worksheet at a time, with arrows to flip to yesterday or tomorrow and a wall calendar to jump anywhere. The worksheet is split into trays (Focus, Schedule, Daily, Weekly, Monthly, One-time and Lists) that can be folded shut when you don't need them. Finished work stays on the sheet with a check mark instead of disappearing. The receptionist understands shorthand like "#fitness workout monday wednesday friday" or "#list groceries: eggs, soap". She notices when two appointments overlap, can move everything unfinished to tomorrow in one go, and keeps the last change in her head for five seconds so an "undo" puts things back exactly as they were. Once a week she writes a short review of what got done, what slipped and where the time went.

What's still missing is the head office. Right now each phone is its own separate shop: there's no ID badge to sign in with, no central filing cabinet, no way to reach Forge's branch office, and nobody to ring you with a reminder. The next phase builds that head office on AWS. The ID-badge office (Cognito) will be shared with Forge, so one badge opens both buildings. Then come a reception desk (API Gateway), on-call clerks (Lambda) and a central filing cabinet (DynamoDB), so the outgoing-mail tray finally has somewhere to deliver to.
