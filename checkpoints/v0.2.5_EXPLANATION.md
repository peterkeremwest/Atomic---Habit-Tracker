# v0.2.5 — What happened, in plain language

Before this version, everything in Atomic belonged to one person. Each list sat in your own drawer of the filing cabinet, and nobody else had a key. Now two people can share a list, which suits errands and shopping. You type `#shared errands: milk, soap`, and the list is filed in a shared drawer in the cabinet instead of your own. The shared drawer is a separate place, and two people hold keys to it. To bring the second person in, you ask for a guest pass, which is a short code or a link. It works once and runs out after 48 hours. The other person enters it after signing in, and the drawer then opens for them too. A shared drawer holds two people at most, and both have full control: either one can add, tick off, remove, rename or delete.

On Today, the shared list shows up in its own SHARED section next to your private items, marked with a small two-person icon, which replaced the "&" that looked confusing. Every ticked item shows the name of whoever ticked it. A second clerk was called in to handle this shared drawer, separate from the data clerk who handles your own items. Each item is filed on its own sheet, so if you tick milk while the other person ticks soap, both ticks are kept and neither overwrites the other. Changes to the list's title or link are checked against a revision number before the clerk files them. If someone else changed the sheet a moment earlier, the clerk refuses the change instead of overwriting it. Your phone still keeps its own copy in its filing drawer and sends changes through the outgoing-mail tray, so a list can still be used with the front desk closed and catches up later.

The second change is links. A web address typed into an item, a step or a note is now tappable. Any list or note can also carry a link button, for example to a store's own shopping list. You add it from the ⋯ menu. On a shared list, both people see the link.

Not changed: the cloud side needs the owner to deploy the new clerk from the terminal, and that step could not be checked from here. What's still ahead: the private PA channel for Forge and Atomic to tell each other about shared fitness goals, and the receptionist's alarm clock with postcards for reminders.
